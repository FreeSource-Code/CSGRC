📝 Submission Specifications & Editorial Standards

Author Submission Guidelines

All manuscripts submitted to Bentham Science must comply rigorously with the following academic standards to qualify for peer review and Scopus indexing.

📄

Word Count

7,000 – 8,000 Words

Full chapters must be comprehensive monographs. The word count covers the entire manuscript, including abstract, introduction, system architecture, evaluation, conclusion, and references.

📑

Formatting & Citation

IEEE Reference Style

Use standard IEEE numerical referencing format [1], [2]. Ensure all references contain full authors, title, journal/conference, DOI, and publication year.

📊

Visuals & Tables

300+ DPI Figures & Tables

Include informative diagrams, flowcharts, and comparative tables. Ensure graphics are vector or 300+ DPI bitmap with clear labels and figure captions.

👤

Author Information

Complete Institutional Affiliations

List all co-authors with full academic titles, departments, university/industry affiliations, city, country, institutional email, and ORCID identifiers.

🤖

AI Policy

Strict No-AI Generation

Chapters generated by Generative AI / Large Language Models (ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) are strictly prohibited. AI usage tools must be disclosed, and intellectual authorship must remain purely human.

🛡️

Originality & Integrity

Less Than 10% Plagiarism

Every submission will undergo rigorous similarity screening via Turnitin / iThenticate. Overall text similarity must remain strictly under 10% (excluding references).

IEEE Reference Style Reference Examples

Authors must ensure their final bibliography conforms to the following IEEE format examples:

[Journal Article]:
[1] J. K. Author, "AI governance frameworks in cloud zero-trust architectures," IEEE Trans. Dependable Secure Comput., vol. 20, no. 4, pp. 1120–1132, 2025.
[Conference Proceeding]:
[2] A. B. Smith and C. D. Jones, "Continuous compliance monitoring using predictive telemetry," in Proc. IEEE Conf. Cybersecur. Privacy (CS&P), 2026, pp. 45–53.
[Book Chapter]:
[3] D. Ather and A. Malhotra, "Zero-Trust Architectures in GRC," in Cybersecurity Governance, Risk, and Compliance, Bentham Science Publishers, 2027, ch. 5, pp. 85–104.
